An independently produced solo exhibition by the Nepalese thangka artist, Najar Man Lama, who initially came to Malaysia as a migrant worker. The exhibition explores Lama's work through migrant labor and cultural displacement, while introducing the visual language of Tibetan Buddhism within a Malaysian context.

Led the exhibition design, research, and curation for the project, beginning with research in Nepal exploring contemporary thangka practices and tracing the artist's journey before his arrival in Malaysia. Developed the spatial layout and interpretive guides, designed all graphic materials, and executed the installation on-site, carrying out the build from painting and lighting to vinyl application.

Visual System

To communicate the process of making a thangka, I graphically deconstructed the painting of the Medicine Buddha and created a five step wall diagram illustrating the thangka painting process. Each stage, (1) Prepare the Canvas, (2) Layout and Proportion, (3) Color and Shading, (4) Finishing and Mounting, and (05) Consecration, is presented on angled panels before the user is directed to the final painting of the Medicine Buddha.

Visual System

Developed a wall-based guide to thangka iconography, explaining symbols and mudras encountered in later rooms. This initial wall functions as a primer, providing the additional context to interpret the paintings that follow.

Interpretive Guides

Thangkas are often used as teaching tools and contain dense symbolic narratives. For works with complex scenes, I developed multilingual interpretive guides in an accordion format, displayed on angled wooden stands positioned directly in front of each painting to support close viewing while reducing text overload.

Translation Guides

The first section of the exhibition contained wall text, presented in English and Malay, reflecting the most widely spoken languages in Malaysia. To support broader accessibility, additional Tamil and Simplified Chinese translations were produced as printed reading guides. The translations were intentionally kept in print rather than digital form, encouraging visitors to engage with the thangkas directly without viewing the works through a phone screen.

Behind the Scenes

Played an active role in the physical build of the exhibition, installing vinyl graphics, painting, and lighting. Involved through final installation and finishing stages.
